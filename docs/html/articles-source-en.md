# Tips and trends: choosing an education path

## 1. Where to start when choosing a university: five questions

**Для карточки:** Country, programme, deadlines, interests and budget: what to decide before you draw up a list of universities.

A beautiful campus helps you picture future life. A place in a ranking gives a point of reference. But neither a photograph nor a ranking answers the main question: does this particular programme suit your child? So that the search does not turn into an endless comparison of names, first agree on five things.

### 1. What does your child want to study and try?

There is no need to pick a lifelong profession right away. To begin with, two or three areas worth exploring are enough. Ask your child for examples: which tasks they enjoy, what they do without being reminded, what they are ready to read about on their own. "I like biology" is a starting point. An interest in laboratory research, working with people or protecting nature already helps tell programmes apart.

Then open the curricula. Compare the compulsory subjects, projects, placements and ways of assessment. Identical programme titles do not guarantee identical content.

### 2. Where and in what language are they ready to study?

Discuss not only the country but everyday life: a big city or a small campus, distance from the family, independent living, the language of communication and teaching. Good spoken language does not yet mean that academic writing will come easily. Check the language proof requirements with the programme itself.

Do not mix up the parents' wishes and the child's readiness. If the child is the one who will study, their doubts must become part of the search criteria.

### 3. Which level of education is needed now?

A bachelor's degree, a preparatory programme and college before university solve different problems. What matters is the document your child finishes school with and whether the chosen university accepts it. For UK programmes, check not only grades but also compulsory subjects: UCAS and the universities state plainly that requirements differ by course.

### 4. Which intake can they realistically prepare for?

Start from the expected year of entry and move backwards. Mark application deadlines, exams, the language certificate, the portfolio and the collection of school documents. For each item write down what is already done and what is still to come. If the requirements are not yet known, do not replace them with the assumption "we will make it".

### 5. What is the full budget?

Count more than the tuition fee. Add housing, food, transport, insurance, trips home, fees and any preparation needed. Write down the scholarship terms separately: until receiving it is confirmed, the budget without it must also be clear to the family.

### What you should end up with

Write a short description of the request: "We are looking for a bachelor's degree in two areas, in this language, for a particular intake, with suitable requirements and within the total budget." On that basis you can already compare programmes and shorten the list.

## 2. What will your child do for a living: how to read demand data

**Для карточки:** Vacancies, salaries and automation: which data help choose a direction and where it is easy to go wrong.

"This profession will be in demand" sounds convincing until the questions begin: in which country, for what level of experience and by what year? Labour market data are useful for comparing options. But they cannot promise a particular teenager a job and an income after graduation.

### First, clarify which job you are comparing

An interest in drawing can lead to illustration, animation, industrial design or interface design. The daily tasks and the ways into these fields differ. So compare specific roles, not broad labels such as "a creative profession".

For each role, collect a small selection of current vacancies in one market. Look separately at offers for beginners: demand for experienced staff does not in itself prove that a newcomer can get hired easily.

### What to check in the data

| Indicator | What to find out | What you cannot conclude |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Number of vacancies | Country, period, platform, duplicates | That every advertisement means a new job |
| Salary range | Currency, experience, type of employment, before or after tax | That the upper limit is a typical graduate income |
| Requirements | Which skills repeat across different advertisements | That a long list of requirements is always mandatory in full |
| Ease of entry | Whether there are internships, junior positions, portfolio requirements | That high overall demand means an easy start |
| Automation | Which exact tasks are changing under the influence of technology | That exposure to AI equals the disappearance of a profession |

Salaries from different countries cannot be compared without the context of living costs and taxes. An unpublished salary is an absence of data, not zero income. Keep the source and date next to every figure.

### What automation risk really says

Research by the International Labour Organization assesses the potential exposure of tasks within occupations to generative AI. It is not a forecast of the actual number of layoffs. A single role may include both automatable operations and work that needs human judgement, responsibility and interaction.

The practical question for a family is: "Which tasks is my child interested in mastering, and how are new tools used in them?" For example, the ability to create an image should be considered together with understanding the client's task, composition, checking the result and working with feedback. This is an example of skills analysis, not a promise of an AI-proof career.

### Test the interest through action

Choose two or three roles and offer a small trial for each: design a mock-up, build a prototype, carry out a study or prepare a short animation. Afterwards discuss what they liked: coming up with ideas, clarifying the task, making things by hand, analysing or refining the result after comments.

It is also useful to talk to a practising specialist: ask about the first working year, typical tasks and the difficulties of getting in. Such a conversation complements the statistics but does not replace them.

## 3. College before university: who this path suits

**Для карточки:** How to choose academic preparation, subjects and environment before applying to university.

The word "college" means different things in different education systems. Here we mean the British sixth form college: a stage of academic preparation before university, usually after GCSEs or a comparable stage of school education. This route cannot be automatically equated with a Russian college after ninth grade. The word college in the UK does not always denote this particular format either.

### First define the goal of the preparation

A college is worth considering when the family needs a clear route to a university programme: to study the necessary subjects, obtain a suitable qualification and prepare for academic independence. Age, previous documents, grades and language must meet the requirements of the chosen institution.

Moving after ninth grade of a Russian school cannot be assumed to be automatically suitable: the possibility of admission and the right stage of study must be confirmed with the specific college.

### Choose subjects with the future programme in mind

First look at the requirements of several university courses, then choose the set of subjects. Otherwise you can finish the preparation well but find that a compulsory subject for the desired programme is missing.

Cambridge, for example, warns directly: the combination of A level or IB subjects affects which university courses are open to you. This is an example of the logic of checking, not a universal requirement of all universities. The conditions of each course must be read separately.

A levels, IB and Foundation should not be treated as interchangeable names. Check which qualification is awarded, by whom it is recognised and where it allows you to apply. A preparatory programme may limit later courses and universities.

### What to compare between colleges

**Curriculum.** The necessary subjects must really be taught in the chosen set. Ask whether they can be combined in the timetable and what conditions apply to changing a subject.

**Support.** Ask who helps with choosing universities, the application, exams and the portfolio, and what exactly is included in the price. The phrase "we support admission" is too general on its own.

**Everyday environment.** Assess group size, independent work, feedback, language support and social life outside lessons. A prestigious environment is not necessarily the right one for this particular teenager.

**Housing and care.** For a minor, find out in advance who is responsible for them outside lessons, how accommodation, contact with the family and help in a difficult situation are arranged. Ask about specific duties, not just the presence of the word "support" in a brochure.

**Total budget.** Add up tuition, housing, food, exam and additional costs. The payment and refund terms must be clear before any money is paid.

### Preparation is not a guarantee of admission

Finishing college does not in itself promise a place at the chosen university. If a transfer through a partner route is advertised, ask for the conditions in writing: which grades, subjects and other requirements must be met and which programmes the offer covers.

## 4. Summer programmes: how to choose by dates and age

**Для карточки:** Age, dates, content and accommodation: how to find a programme that suits your child and your family.

Two summer programmes can have equally beautiful photographs and a completely different experience inside. On one, your child will try a new subject; on another, they will study a language intensively; on a third, they will spend most of the time on excursions. Start with what result is needed right now.

### Choose one main goal

Discuss with your child: do they want to test their interest in a field, complete a project, practise a language or try living in an international environment? Several goals can be combined, but one should drive the choice. For getting to know a subject, real assignments and feedback matter. For language practice, what matters is how often the child will speak and with whom.

### Check the age on the right date

The organiser may have different rules for calculating age: at the start of the session or on another fixed date. Do not draw a conclusion from the range on the card alone. If your child is on the border of an age group, get confirmation before paying. Not every university summer school accepts minors: for example, some Oxford Lifelong Learning programmes are for adults only.

### Match the study dates with the trip

The start date of classes may differ from the arrival day. Note separately the arrival, the study period, the departure, the transfer and a time buffer for travel. Check overlaps with school exams and family plans. If documents or the trip need preparation, take it into account before booking non-refundable services.

### Look at the schedule of an ordinary day

Ask for a sample week: how many lessons, independent work, sports, excursions and free time. Find out the group size, the language of instruction and the expected starting level. The wording "design classes" says little about the content; it is clearer when it states what the child will create and what feedback they will receive.

### Find out who runs the programme

The name of a city or a photograph of a campus does not prove that the organiser is a university. Check the organiser's legal name, the teachers and the nature of the link with the venue. Participation and a certificate should not be taken as a promise of advantages in future admission: judge first of all by the content and the experience.

### Run a short check before paying

- The child's age and level meet the rules of participation.
- The exact arrival and departure dates are confirmed.
- The study load and the final result are clear.
- It is known where the child lives and who is responsible for them outside lessons.
- Meals, medical help and emergency contact are clarified.
- There is a full list of included and additional costs.
- The terms of cancellation, refund and replacement of a session have been read.
- The child understands where they are going and wants to take part.

### How to tell whether the programme delivered

Before the trip, write down one question your child wants to answer: "Do I like doing research?" or "Am I ready to study in English every day?" Afterwards discuss the answer and specific examples. Even the conclusion "this direction is not for me" can help the next educational decision.
